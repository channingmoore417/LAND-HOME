If you're thinking about moving to Lake Charles, there's a good chance something brought you here first.

Maybe it's a new job. Maybe you're coming to work at one of the LNG or industrial projects around Southwest Louisiana. Maybe you're taking a position at one of our hospitals, McNeese, a local plant, or one of the hundreds of companies that support them.

Or maybe you have family here, visited a few times, and started wondering what it would actually be like to live here.

Lake Charles isn't a huge city, and that's part of what people like about it.

You can live in a neighborhood with old oak trees near the center of town, buy a newer home farther south, find a place on the water, or move a little farther out and get more land. You can keep a boat in the driveway and be fishing after work. You can get across most of town without planning your whole day around traffic.

And even though Lake Charles isn't a major metro, there is a lot happening here.

Southwest Louisiana is home to some of the largest energy and industrial projects in the country. We have McNeese State University, major hospitals, two large casino resorts, a busy construction industry, a growing LNG corridor, and a downtown and lakefront that have changed quite a bit over the years.

But the things that make Lake Charles feel like home aren't really the things you can put on a spreadsheet.

It's Saturday crawfish boils. High school football. McNeese games. Fishing Big Lake. Mardi Gras. Gumbo when it finally gets cold. Knowing somebody who knows somebody everywhere you go. Hurricane stories. Sunday dinners. Boats, camps, hunting leases, family gatherings, and an argument over who has the best boudin.

That's Southwest Louisiana.

If you're seriously thinking about moving here, though, there are some things worth understanding before you pick a house.

Lake Charles, Sulphur, Carlyss, Moss Bluff, Westlake, and Iowa may all look close together on a map, but they don't give you the same day-to-day life. Where you work matters. Schools matter. Insurance matters. And in this part of Louisiana, which side of the river you live on can matter more than a newcomer would ever guess.

So this isn't a list of reasons you should move here.

It's a guide to help you understand the place before you do.

## First, "Lake Charles" Usually Means More Than Lake Charles

This is probably the easiest thing for someone new to the area to misunderstand.

When locals talk about the Lake Charles area, we aren't always talking about the city limits.

Lake Charles is the center of Southwest Louisiana, but the communities around it are tied together. People live in Sulphur and work in Lake Charles. They live in Moss Bluff and shop in Lake Charles. They live in Carlyss and tell somebody from out of state they live "around Lake Charles" because that's easier than explaining where Carlyss is.

The city itself had about 81,000 people in the Census Bureau's latest estimate, but Lake Charles is the hub for a metro area of roughly 245,000.

That broader picture matters when you start looking for a home.

If you only type "Lake Charles" into a home search, you may miss an area that would actually fit your life better.

None of the towns around it is automatically better than the others.

A lot comes down to where you're going every morning.

## What Does Lake Charles Actually Feel Like?

This is a hard question to answer with statistics.

Lake Charles is big enough that you don't feel like you live in the middle of nowhere, but small enough that you aren't swallowed by the city.

You can go to Target, grab dinner, get your kids to practice, and get home without driving 45 minutes between every stop.

There are parts of town that feel suburban, parts that feel older and established, historic streets near downtown, newer developments farther south, and plenty of places where you're only a few minutes from open land or water.

The city is tied closely to the water.

That's easy to forget if you've only looked at Lake Charles on Google Maps.

There's the lake itself, the Calcasieu River, Prien Lake, bayous, canals, marsh, and then the Gulf farther south. Boats aren't just toys here. For a lot of people they're part of life.

The Creole Nature Trail runs from the Lake Charles area through the marshes of Cameron Parish and down to 26 miles of natural Gulf beaches. The route passes through some of the best fishing, crabbing, birding, and wildlife country in Louisiana.

If you like being outside, you'll have plenty to do.

If your idea of outdoor life is hiking mountains every weekend, we're going to disappoint you.

Our mountains are bridges.

But if you like boats, fishing, hunting, crabbing, kayaking, marshes, wildlife, or being near the water, this part of Louisiana is hard to beat.

## The Food Thing Is Real

People make fun of Louisiana for talking about food all the time until they live here for six months.

Then they start doing it too.

Food isn't just something you go out and buy in Southwest Louisiana. It is tied into almost everything.

Someone has a birthday? We're cooking.

Football game? We're cooking.

Church event? Definitely cooking.

A little cold front shows up? Somebody is making gumbo.

Crawfish are in season? Plans for Saturday are pretty much made.

Boudin, cracklins, gumbo, étouffée, fried seafood, boiled seafood, smoked sausage and plate lunches aren't special-occasion "Cajun food" here. They're normal.

And some of the best things you'll eat will come from a place that absolutely does not look impressive from the road.

That's part of the fun.

## Mardi Gras Here Isn't Just a New Orleans Thing

If you're moving from outside Louisiana, you may think Mardi Gras means Bourbon Street.

That's not really what Mardi Gras looks like here.

Southwest Louisiana has its own Mardi Gras traditions, krewes, parades, gumbo cookoffs and family events. Lake Charles' major celebrations are centered around downtown and the Event Center, and the season runs for weeks leading up to Mardi Gras Day.

You'll also find festivals and events throughout the year.

Some are big enough to take over downtown or the lakefront. Others are the kind of small-town festivals where half the fun is running into people you know.

You don't have to make any of it your personality.

But it doesn't take long to realize that people here like getting together.

## Where You Work May Decide Where You Should Live

This is one of the first conversations we have with people moving here.

Not:

"What neighborhood do you like?"

First:

"Where are you going to work?"

Because those two questions are connected.

Lake Charles sits east of the Calcasieu River. Sulphur, Westlake, Carlyss and much of the industrial corridor sit west of it.

If you work at Memorial Hospital or McNeese, your housing search may look very different from somebody working at one of the plants west of the river.

And if you're driving south toward one of the LNG projects, it changes again.

This doesn't mean crossing the bridge is some terrible daily event. Thousands of people do it.

It just means you shouldn't ignore your commute when you're deciding where to buy.

A house that looks 15 miles from work on a map may not feel like the best location after you've driven the route twice a day for a year.

That's especially true if you're working 10 or 12-hour shifts.

## So Where Should You Live?

This is where we'd rather give you choices than declare one part of town "the best."

Because there isn't one.

If you want to be close to most of the shopping and restaurants and have plenty of different housing options, [Lake Charles itself](/lake-charles/homes-for-sale) is the obvious place to start.

South Lake Charles has seen a lot of residential growth over the years. You'll find established subdivisions, newer neighborhoods and communities like [Graywood](/homes-for-sale?city=Lake%20Charles&neighborhood=graywood) and [Morganfield](/homes-for-sale?city=Lake%20Charles&neighborhood=morganfield) in that direction.

If you like older homes and more character, look closer to the center of town.

The [Charpentier Historic District](/homes-for-sale?city=Lake%20Charles&neighborhood=charpentier-historic-district) and [Margaret Place](/homes-for-sale?city=Lake%20Charles&neighborhood=margaret-place) have a completely different feel from the newer neighborhoods farther south. You get older architecture, mature trees and better access to downtown and the lakefront.

There are also plenty of established neighborhoods around McNeese and other parts of the city where you'll find brick homes on mature lots that don't fit neatly into either "historic" or "new construction."

Then there's [waterfront](/lake-charles/waterfront-homes).

Lake Charles is actually a place where buying a home on a lake, bayou or canal is a real option. What that looks like and what it costs varies a lot, but if being on the water matters to you, tell your agent that early.

## And Don't Be Surprised if You End Up Outside the City

A lot of relocation buyers start by asking us about Lake Charles and end up falling in love with somewhere else.

[Sulphur](/sulphur/homes-for-sale) is probably the easiest example.

It's not just a subdivision outside Lake Charles. It's its own city, with its own restaurants, stores, parks, schools, neighborhoods, and employers.

If you work west or south of Lake Charles, living in Sulphur can make your drive much easier while still giving you grocery stores, restaurants, parks and everything else you need for normal life.

[Carlyss](/carlyss/homes-for-sale) is south of Sulphur and more spread out, and it puts you closer to much of the industrial and LNG work southwest of town. It can be a great fit if you want a little more breathing room and don't mind doing most of your shopping in Sulphur.

[Moss Bluff](/moss-bluff/homes-for-sale) is north of Lake Charles and has always been popular with people who like bigger lots, more space and a more suburban-to-rural feel.

[Westlake](/westlake/homes-for-sale) is smaller and sits right across the river from Lake Charles.

[Iowa](/iowa/homes-for-sale) is east of town along I-10 and can make sense if you're looking for land, newer construction or simply prefer being outside the city.

That's why we don't like telling people to choose an area before we know anything about them.

## Please Get an Insurance Quote Before You Fall in Love With the House

If you're moving here from another part of the country, the home insurance conversation may be different from what you're used to.

Southwest Louisiana is hurricane country.

That isn't a reason to be afraid of buying a house here. It is a reason to understand what you're buying.

Two homes with the same asking price can have different insurance costs because of the age of the house, roof, location, construction, claims history and other factors.

Flood insurance is also separate from a standard homeowners policy.

So before you decide one house is a better deal than another, look at the whole monthly picture.

Ask about the flood zone. Our [Lake Charles flood zones guide](/blog/lake-charles-flood-zones-explained) explains what the zones mean.

Ask whether there is an elevation certificate.

Get a homeowners quote.

Find out what the hurricane or named-storm deductible is.

Look at the roof.

Those aren't fun parts of shopping for a house, but they matter here.

People in Southwest Louisiana have lived with hurricanes for generations. You learn to prepare, pay attention to forecasts, know your insurance coverage and take storms seriously when one is coming.

That's part of living near the Gulf.

It isn't the whole story of living here.

## Yes, Summer Is Hot

There's no clever way to dress this one up.

July is hot.

August is hot.

Sometimes September forgets that fall is supposed to exist.

Lake Charles' normal summer afternoon temperatures are around the low 90s, and the humidity makes those numbers feel plenty warm. Winters, on the other hand, are generally mild.

You are going to use your air conditioner a lot.

That's normal.

The tradeoff is that winter is usually short, boating and fishing don't disappear for half the year, and there are a lot of days between October and spring that remind you why people live in Louisiana.

Every once in a while we get genuinely cold weather and collectively behave like the Arctic Circle moved to Calcasieu Parish.

Then three days later somebody is wearing shorts again.

## If You Have Kids, Check the Address, Not Just the Neighborhood Name

Most public schools in the area are part of the Calcasieu Parish School Board.

CPSB serves more than 29,000 students across more than 50 schools.

One thing relocation buyers need to know is that school attendance zones don't always follow the neighborhood names you see on a listing.

The school attached to a house is based on the actual address.

CPSB has an official **Find My School Zone** tool on the [Calcasieu Parish School Board website](https://www.cpsb.org), and that's what we recommend using when a particular school matters to your move.

Don't rely only on an old listing, Zillow, or what somebody remembers from five years ago.

Check the address.

There are also private-school choices in the area, but that's a separate conversation and one we don't need to cram into a paragraph here.

## Lake Charles Is Still Very Much a Working Town

There are places where the economy revolves around tourism.

Lake Charles has tourism, but that's not what built this area.

Industry is a huge part of Southwest Louisiana.

Petrochemical plants, refineries, LNG, contractors, fabrication, marine work and all of the companies that support those industries employ a lot of people here.

Then you have healthcare, McNeese State University, SOWELA, local schools, government, casinos, hospitality, small businesses and everything else that keeps a regional city running.

That mix is one reason people continue moving into the area for work.

You will notice the industrial side of Lake Charles when you live here.

Drive west toward Sulphur and Westlake and you can't miss it.

Drive south toward Cameron Parish and you see another side of the same economy.

For a lot of local families, those plants and projects aren't something happening in the background. They are where parents, grandparents, spouses, friends and neighbors have built careers.

## Getting Around Town Is Pretty Simple Once You Learn It

There are a few roads you'll hear constantly.

I-10 runs east and west through the north side of Lake Charles.

I-210 loops around the south side.

Ryan Street runs north and south through the middle of town.

Nelson Road is one of the main roads through south Lake Charles.

Highway 171 takes you north toward Moss Bluff.

Highway 27 runs south through the Sulphur/Carlyss side toward Cameron Parish.

Within Lake Charles itself, getting around is usually straightforward.

Traffic absolutely exists.

School traffic exists.

Plant shift changes exist.

Anybody who has been stuck trying to get somewhere at the wrong time can tell you Lake Charles is not magically traffic-free.

But if you're coming from Houston, Dallas or another major metro, the scale is completely different.

The one thing newcomers tend to underestimate is how much the river can affect the way they think about town.

Once you know where you work and where you spend most of your time, the map starts making a lot more sense.

## What Do People Actually Do Here?

A lot depends on what you like.

If you're outdoorsy, Southwest Louisiana gives you a lot.

You can fish freshwater in the morning and saltwater without leaving the region. Big Lake, the Calcasieu River, the marshes, bayous and Gulf all create different places to fish.

People hunt.

They crab.

They boat.

They kayak.

They head down the Creole Nature Trail.

There are state parks and local parks, and Prien Lake Park is one of those places where you'll see everything from kids playing to people walking at sunset.

If you're more interested in entertainment, Lake Charles has the Golden Nugget and L'Auberge casino resorts, concerts, restaurants, local music, McNeese sports and events downtown.

And Houston is close enough that people regularly head west for professional sports, concerts, shopping, flights or a weekend away.

Lake Charles Regional Airport also has commercial service connecting through Houston and Dallas-Fort Worth, which is useful when you don't feel like starting every trip with a drive to Houston.

You aren't going to find everything a city of several million people has.

Most people moving here know that.

What surprises some of them is how much there actually is for a city this size.

## Is Lake Charles a Good Place to Live?

For a lot of us, absolutely.

But that's a personal question.

If you want skyscrapers, public transportation everywhere, four professional sports teams and a different national touring act every night, this probably isn't the city you're looking for.

If you like having room, being near the water, eating ridiculously well, knowing your neighbors, spending less time sitting in traffic, and living in a place that still feels connected to its own culture, Lake Charles has a lot going for it.

We have hot summers.

We have storms.

We have potholes.

We have bridges that everybody has an opinion about.

And after a hurricane, you'll probably learn more about your neighbors in three days than you did in the previous three years.

That's part of this place too.

Lake Charles has been through a lot, especially in the last several years.

People rebuilt homes, reopened businesses, planted trees again and kept going.

You can see scars from storms in parts of town.

You can also see a city that's still growing, building and figuring out what its next chapter looks like.

That's a much more honest picture of Lake Charles than pretending everything here is perfect.

And it's also why a lot of us wouldn't want to live anywhere else.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Lake Charles a good place to live?
For a lot of people, yes. You get room, the water, great food, a strong job market and a lot less traffic than a big metro. You also get hot summers and hurricane season. If you want a big-city lifestyle, it probably isn't the right fit. If you want space and a place with its own culture, it has a lot going for it.

### How far is Lake Charles from Houston?
About two hours east on I-10, depending on traffic. People here head to Houston regularly for sports, concerts, shopping and flights.

### Where should I live if I'm moving to Lake Charles?
Start with where you'll be working. Lake Charles has the most choices, but Sulphur, Carlyss and Westlake sit on the west side of the river closer to much of the industrial corridor, and Moss Bluff and Iowa give you more room. The best area is the one that fits your drive, your budget and how much land you want.

### Do I need flood insurance in Lake Charles?
If the home is in a high-risk flood zone and you have a federally backed mortgage, yes. In lower-risk zones it's usually optional, but a lot of people carry it anyway. Either way, ask about the flood zone and get quotes before you decide on a house.

### How do I find out which school a house is zoned for?
Use the Calcasieu Parish School Board's Find My School Zone tool and check the exact address. School zones don't always follow neighborhood names, so don't rely on a listing or what someone remembers.

### Is it hard to get around Lake Charles?
Not once you learn a few roads. I-10, I-210, Ryan Street, Nelson Road, Highway 171 and Highway 27 will get you most places. Traffic exists at school times and shift changes, but it's nothing like Houston or Dallas.
